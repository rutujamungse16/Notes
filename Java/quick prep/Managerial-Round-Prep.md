**Q19. What's a technical decision you're proud of, and why?**

**Q20. How do you handle ambiguity in requirements?**
A: I don't wait for perfect clarity — I make reasonable assumptions, document them explicitly, and confirm with the stakeholder early, rather than blocking progress or guessing silently and building the wrong thing.

**Q21. Tell me about a time you had to make a trade-off between speed and quality.**
A: During a sprint with a tight deadline, rather than skip testing, I focused my testing effort on the highest-risk logic (data transformation correctness) and deferred lower-risk polish items to a fast-follow — communicating that trade-off transparently to my lead rather than cutting corners silently.

**Q22. How do you keep your technical skills current?**

## Section 5: Problem-Solving & Decision-Making (Q47–Q58)

**Q47. Describe your approach to solving a complex, ambiguous problem.**
A: Break it into smaller known/unknown pieces, tackle the highest-risk unknown first (to fail fast if it won't work), validate assumptions with stakeholders or data, and iterate rather than trying to solve it entirely upfront.

**Q48. Tell me about a time you made a decision with incomplete information.**
A: During migration work, when legacy documentation was incomplete, I made a reasonable interpretation based on patterns in the data itself, clearly flagged it as an assumption to the client, and built in validation checks so any wrong assumption would surface early rather than silently causing data issues.

**Q49. How do you prioritize when you have multiple competing deadlines?**
A: I assess business impact and dependencies — what blocks others gets priority — and communicate trade-offs transparently to my lead rather than trying to silently do everything and risking quality on all fronts.

**Q50. Describe a time you identified a problem before it became critical.**
A: I noticed early in a migration project that data volume was higher than initially scoped, which would have caused performance issues at the original approach — I raised it early and we adjusted the transformation strategy (batching) before it became a production issue.

**Q51. How do you approach root cause analysis for a recurring issue?**
A: I look past the immediate symptom to patterns — e.g., if similar bugs keep surfacing in a specific module, I check if it's a testing gap, unclear requirement pattern, or design issue, and fix the systemic cause, not just the instance.

**Q52. Tell me about a time your initial solution didn't work. What did you do?**
A: On a performance issue with a high-volume query, my first indexing attempt didn't fully resolve the slowness — I went back to the execution plan, found the real bottleneck was a join order issue, and restructured the query, which resolved it.

**Q53. How do you balance perfectionism with pragmatic delivery?**
A: I aim for "production-ready," not "perfect" — focusing rigor on things that actually matter (data integrity, security, core logic) and being pragmatic on lower-risk areas, especially under sprint deadlines.

**Q54. Describe a time you had to solve a problem outside your usual area of expertise.**
A: When frontend-backend integration issues came up on the pharmacy management system project, despite being backend-focused, I dug into the React side enough to identify whether the issue was API contract mismatch or frontend state handling, which sped up resolution instead of just waiting on someone else.

**Q55. How do you decide when to escalate a problem versus solving it yourself?**
A: If it's within my control and doesn't affect other teams/timelines significantly, I solve it. If it involves cross-team dependencies, risk to the client, or a decision beyond my authority, I escalate early rather than late.

**Q56. Tell me about a time you used data to make a decision.**
A: I used query execution plans to decide where to add indexes rather than guessing based on intuition — this ensured the optimization actually addressed the real bottleneck instead of a perceived one.

**Q57. How do you handle a situation with no clear "right" answer?**
A: I lay out the trade-offs of each option clearly, use whatever data/context is available to lean toward the option with the best risk/reward balance, and stay ready to adjust if new information emerges.

**Q58. Describe your process for evaluating a new tool or approach before adopting it.**
A: I check if it solves an actual pain point we have (not just because it's new), do a small proof-of-concept, and validate it against real project constraints before recommending broader adoption.
